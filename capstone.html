A subscription business asks you to predict which customers are about to cancel, so that someone can ring them first. You have an archive of 600 past customers. Make the decisions on the left. The lab trains the model your way and shows the score you would have reported, and then what it does on 4,000 customers it has never seen.

Case study: entirely fictional and synthetic. Three honest signals (tenure, support tickets, recent logins), forty columns of noise, and one column that should worry you. The model is the single neuron from lesson 03 with more inputs. About one customer in six cancels. There is no answer key: every check below is computed from what your model actually does.

The model card

One page that says what the model is for, what went into it, how it was scored and what it cannot do. Writing it honestly is most of the discipline. 


            

          

        

      

      

        

          
Honest scoring

          

            Lesson 02 at work: score on customers the model never saw, and keep
            a final set that no choice was tuned against.
          

        

        

          
Honest inputs

          

            Every input must exist at the moment of prediction. A column filled
            in afterwards is the answer in disguise, and the most common way
            real projects fool themselves.
          

        

        

          
Honest measures

          

            Lesson 03 at work: when one outcome is rare, accuracy flatters.
            Measure what the business needs, and compare it with doing nothing.
