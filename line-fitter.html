The map is the machine's whole world

A learning algorithm never sees the scatter plot. It sees only the height of the ground where it stands. Everything in the next lab happens on the right-hand chart.

Examples shape the ground

Drag one example and the whole landscape shifts. Training data does not just score the model. It defines what “good” means.

How you measure the miss is a choice

Squaring punishes big misses hard, so one odd example can drag the line. Plain distance shrugs it off. Neither is correct; they encode different priorities.

Going further

The squared-miss fit is ordinary least squares, first published by Legendre in 1805. For a careful treatment see Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman, The Elements of Statistical Learning, chapter 3; for a gentler one, James et al., An Introduction to Statistical Learning, chapter 3. Both are free to read online. The examples here are synthetic; nothing in this lab is a claim about real rents.
