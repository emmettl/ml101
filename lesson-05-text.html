Lesson 05 · Representation

Text becomes numbers

Every model so far has eaten numbers: a flat's size, a dot's position. Language is not numbers. Two steps fix that. First, cut the text into pieces from a fixed list. Then give each piece a position in space, and let training move the positions until words used alike end up close together.

The one idea

Cut text into pieces, then give every piece a place

A network needs a fixed menu of inputs. Whole words make a poor menu: there are millions, new ones appear daily, and “run”, “runs” and “running” would be unrelated entries. Single letters make a tiny menu but force the model to relearn spelling before it can learn anything else. The working compromise is pieces of words, chosen by counting. Frequent words become one piece. Rare words are spelled out of smaller ones. These pieces are tokens, and a model never sees anything else.

A token is still only an entry number, and entry 4,117 is not “more” than entry 52. So each token is given a list of numbers, its coordinates, which start random and are trained like any other knobs. The pressure that shapes them is simple: tokens that turn up in similar company are pushed towards similar coordinates. The result is an embedding: a map on which distance means difference in use.

Nobody tells the model what any word means. It learns where each word belongs from the company that word keeps, and position does the work that a definition would.
Token

A piece of text from a fixed list of tens of thousands. Often a word, often less.

Tokeniser

The rule that cuts text into tokens. Built once, by counting, before training begins.

Embedding

A token's coordinates. Hundreds or thousands of trained numbers per token.

Dimension

One coordinate. More dimensions give meaning more independent directions to vary in.

Step one

Build a vocabulary by gluing together whatever is common

Start with single characters. Find the pair of neighbours that occurs most often in a large body of text and glue them into one new piece. Do it again, counting the new piece like any other. After a few hundred rounds “the” is one piece, “ing” is one piece, and “Alice” is one piece if your text is Alice in Wonderland, which here it is. Move the slider to replay the process.

    Step two, and the arithmetic

    Meaning turns out to have directions

    The figure below was trained on an invented mini-language of 900 three- and four-word sentences, in which kings and queens appear near “crown” and “throne”, women and girls near “she” and “her”, and so on. Every word began at a random point in eight dimensions. Nothing in training mentions royalty or gender. Yet afterwards one direction in the space separates male from female, and another, at right angles to it, separates royal from common. The picture shows the eight people placed by those two directions.

    Because meaning has become geometry, you can do arithmetic with it. Take the step from “man” to “king”, which is whatever royalty adds, and apply the same step starting at “woman”. You land beside “queen”. Nobody programmed that. It falls out of positions learned from neighbours, and it is the clearest evidence that these coordinates encode something real about how words are used.

    The numbers at scale

    A large model has around 100,000 tokens and several thousand dimensions: hundreds of millions of knobs spent on the map alone, before any thinking happens.

    Why so many dimensions?

    Two dimensions cannot keep gender, rank, age, animal and food all independent. In the lab this same arithmetic fails about half the time in 2-D and never in 8-D.

    Takeaways

    What to remember

    Models read tokens, not letters or words

    The vocabulary is fixed in advance by counting what is common.

    An embedding is a learned position

    More knobs, trained by the same descent as everything else.

    Meaning is company kept

    Words used alike land close together. That is all “understanding” starts from.

    Directions carry meaning

    Which is why the space needs many dimensions, and why biases in text become geometry too.
