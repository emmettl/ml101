Lesson 14 · Other machines

Drawing by removing noise

Lesson 08 said that image generators use the same knobs, loss and descent as everything else, with a task of removing noise from a picture a little at a time. This lesson makes good on that in two dimensions, where a picture is a cloud of points and you can watch a shape drown and then be drawn back out of nothing.

The one idea

Learn to clean a little, then clean a lot

Making a picture from nothing is a hopeless training target: which picture? Removing a little noise from a picture is an easy one, and it has a right answer, because you added the noise yourself. A diffusion model is a network trained on that easy job, run many times over.

Training goes like this. Take an example. Mix in noise, a chosen amount on a fixed schedule from “barely any” to “nothing but”. Show the network the result and how far along the schedule it is, and ask it to guess the noise you added. Score the guess with lesson 00's squared error and nudge every knob, as always. Drawing goes the other way. Start from pure noise. Ask the network what noise it sees, subtract a little of it, add a pinch of fresh noise, and ask again, sixty times. What is left is a new example that was never in the training set.

Drown examples in noise on a schedule. Train a network to guess the noise. Draw by starting from pure noise and removing the guessed noise a step at a time. A picture generator is this, with pixels for points.
The schedule

How much signal survives at each stage, fixed in advance and told to the network. Early stages keep almost everything; the last keeps nothing.

The target

Not the clean example but the noise itself. Guessing the noise and guessing the example are the same information, and the noise is the steadier target to learn.

Why steps

From pure noise there is no right answer, only a direction. Small steps with a little fresh noise let the drawing commit gradually instead of guessing everything at once.

Steering

Give the network a caption's embedding as an extra input during training, and at drawing time the caption steers which picture the noise becomes.

The mechanism

Drown a shape, stage by stage

Four hundred points on a shape, and the schedule the lab uses. Slide through the sixty stages and watch how long the shape stays recognisable, and where it is finally gone. The network is trained at every stage at once; the drawing runs from the last stage back to the first.

The trick of the schedule is that neighbouring stages differ only a little, so undoing one stage is always a modest ask. Undoing all sixty at once would mean drawing the whole shape from pure noise in a single guess, which is the hopeless job the method was invented to avoid.

The arithmetic, and what it does not buy

Sixty guesses per picture, and a picture that was never seen

A language model makes one forward pass per token. A diffusion model makes one per step per picture, sixty here and often twenty to fifty in products, each a full run of a large network over every pixel. That is why generating a picture takes seconds and why so much engineering goes into needing fewer steps.

What comes out was never in the training set, and is not a copy of anything, in the same sense that lesson 07's text was not: the network learned what the examples have in common, and draws from that. It is also in the same sense a mirror of its examples. Whatever was common in them is common in what it draws, and whatever was absent it cannot draw at all.

Takeaways

What to remember

Easy job, many times

Removing a little noise has a right answer. Drawing is that job repeated from pure noise to a finished picture.

Knobs, loss, descent, again

Squared error on a noise guess, nudged by descent. Nothing new inside; only the task is new.

Steps are why it works

From pure noise the only honest guess is an average. Gradual steps let a drawing commit.

Mirror of its examples

New pictures, drawn from what the training pictures had in common. Lesson 05's warning holds for pictures too.
