Lesson 13 · Other machines

Learning from consequences

A chess program is never told the right move. A robot is never told the right joint angle. They are told, eventually, whether things went well. Reinforcement learning is learning from that: rewards that arrive late, for actions whose share of the credit nobody has worked out. It is also the third stage of training a chatbot, which is why it ends this course.

The one idea

Guess the reward to come, then correct the guess

The agent's knobs are guesses: for every situation and every move it might make, how much reward is still to come. Pick the move with the best guess, make it, and see what happens. Now the guess can be corrected, not against a label, because there is none, but against what actually happened plus the guess for the situation you landed in. Nudge it a fraction of the way towards that. Repeat, for a lifetime of moves.

That nudge is lesson 01's descent on a squared error, with one twist: the target is made partly of the agent's own guesses. Good news spreads backwards a step at a time. The move that reached the goal is praised first; the move before it, on the next visit; and so on down the route, which is how an action nine moves before the reward eventually gets its share of the credit.

Reinforcement learning: keep a guess of the reward to come for every situation and move, act on the best guess, and correct it towards what happened plus the next guess. The credit for a late reward flows backwards one step per visit.
Reward, not label

A label says what was right. A reward says how it went. The agent must work out which of its moves to thank, and which to blame.

Explore or exploit

Always taking the best guess means never discovering a better move. Some share of moves are made at random, and every one of them can cost.

Discount

A reward next move counts for more than one ten moves away. The rate at which it fades sets how far ahead the agent cares.

The table becomes a network

A table of guesses works for a grid. For a screen of pixels or a sentence, lesson 04's network guesses instead, trained by the same nudge.

Switch the agent to SARSA and it moves away from the edge. The two differ in one line. Q-learning corrects a guess as if the next move will be the best one; SARSA corrects it using the move the agent will actually make, random ones included. Q-learning therefore learns the best route for an agent that never slips, and keeps slipping. SARSA learns the best route for the agent it is. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions.

The arithmetic, and what it does not buy

The reward is the whole specification

Nothing in the agent knows what a cliff is. It knows that some moves cost a hundred. Change the numbers and it changes its route, with perfect indifference to what you meant. Agents given a reward for “points” in a boat race learned to circle a lagoon collecting bonuses instead of finishing; agents rewarded for speed learned to fall over quickly. The reward is a contract, and the agent reads it as literally as a contract can be read.

Lesson 08's third stage is this, with people as the reward. A second model learns to predict which of two replies people prefer, and the chatbot's knobs are nudged towards replies that score well. It learns what was rewarded, which is what people preferred, which is not always what was true. That is the contract, read literally, again.

Takeaways

What to remember

Guess, act, correct

The knobs are guesses of reward to come, nudged towards what happened plus the next guess. Descent, with a moving target.

Credit flows backwards slowly

A late reward reaches the early moves one visit at a time. Learning from consequences takes many consequences.

Exploring costs, and cannot be skipped

Random moves find better routes and fall off cliffs. Which agent you are learning for decides where to walk.

The reward is read literally

Whatever pays is what gets done. Writing the reward is the hard part, for robots and for chatbots alike.
