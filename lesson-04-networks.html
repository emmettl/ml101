Lesson 04 · Neural networks

Stacking neurons

One neuron draws one line and fails on opposite corners. Feed several neurons into another neuron and the lines combine into corners, rings and spirals. That is a neural network. Nothing else new is needed except a way to work out which knob to blame.

The one idea

Let neurons vote on each other's answers

Put a row of neurons side by side, all looking at the same two inputs. Each learns its own line and reports how far the example sits from it. Now add one more neuron whose inputs are not the raw measurements but those reports. It can say “triangle if the example is above line one and below line two”, which is a corner. With enough lines to combine, any outline can be approximated.

The middle row is called a hidden layer, because nobody tells it what its lines should be. No example says “neuron three, you handle the top-left corner”. The layer works out a useful division of labour for itself, purely because doing so lowers the loss. Stack more layers and later ones combine the shapes made by earlier ones.

A neural network is neurons feeding neurons. The early ones carve the input into simple pieces. The later ones assemble the pieces into an answer.

Opposite corners fall to two or three hidden neurons. The ring needs exactly three, since three lines is the fewest that can enclose anything. This gentle spiral also yields to three, a reminder that these are small, clean puzzles. In each case there is a point beyond which extra neurons add nothing, because the pattern is already captured. On clean patterns the spare capacity is harmless. On noisy data it is exactly what lesson 02 warned about.

The arithmetic

Blame flows backwards

Descent needs a slope for every knob. For the output neuron that is easy: its error is the guess minus the answer. But how wrong was hidden neuron three? It has no right answer to compare against.

The resolution is to pass the blame back along the wires. The output's error is shared among the hidden neurons in proportion to the weights connecting them: a neuron the output listened to closely takes more of the blame. Each hidden neuron then treats its share as its own error and does what any neuron does. With more layers, repeat. One sweep forwards to make a guess, one sweep backwards to distribute blame, and every knob in the network has its slope.

Counting knobs

Two inputs into 8 neurons is 16 weights and 8 biases. Those 8 into one output is 8 weights and a bias. Total: 33.

The same sum, larger

A large language model is this arithmetic with hundreds of billions of knobs. The update rule is unchanged: knob ← knob − rate × slope.

Takeaways

What to remember

Networks are neurons feeding neurons

Simple pieces first, combinations of pieces later.

Hidden roles are discovered

Nothing labels the middle. It organises itself to lower the loss.

Backpropagation shares out the blame

Forwards for the guess, backwards for the slopes, then the usual step downhill.

Size is capacity

Enough neurons to capture the pattern, and lesson 02 applies to every one beyond that.
