Attention

An embedding gives a word one fixed position. But “bank” by a river and “bank” with a vault are different things, and “it” means nothing until you know what it points at. Attention is how each word gets to look at the others and adjust itself to fit. It is the “T” in GPT.

The one idea

Every word asks a question, and listens to whoever answers best

Picture the words of a sentence at a party. Each one wears a badge saying what it is: “I am a living thing”, “I am a place”, “I am just grammar”. Each one also has something it wants to find out. The word “it” wants to know: which of you am I standing in for?

“It” compares its question with every badge in the room. Where question and badge match well, it listens closely; where they do not, it barely listens. Then it rewrites itself as a blend of what it heard, weighted by how closely it listened. After that step “it” is no longer a vague pronoun. It carries most of the meaning of “animal”.

Attention is a weighted average. The weights come from how well one word's question matches each other word's badge. Everything else is bookkeeping.
Query

The question a word asks. A list of numbers, computed from the word by learned knobs.

Key

The badge a word wears. Also numbers, also computed by learned knobs.

Score, then share

Query times key gives a match score. A squashing step, softmax, turns the scores into shares that add up to 100%.

Value

What a word hands over when listened to. The asker becomes the share-weighted mix.

With the focus at zero every word gets an equal share and “it” becomes an average of the whole sentence: a bag of words, with no idea who did what. With the focus very high it listens to one word and ignores the rest, which is brittle. Trained models settle in between, and they learn where, because focus is set by knobs like everything else.

The arithmetic, and the rest of a transformer

Do that for every word, many times over

In this lesson the questions and badges were set by hand. In a real model three small sets of learned weights turn each word's embedding into its query, its key and its value. Nobody tells them what to ask. As in lesson 04, useful questions emerge because they lower the loss.

Every word at once

All words ask their questions in parallel. That is a great many multiplications of the same kind, which is exactly what graphics chips are built for.

Many heads

Each layer runs dozens of attentions side by side, free to specialise: one tracks what pronouns refer to, another which verb a noun belongs to.

Many layers

Attention, then a small network from lesson 04 applied to each word, repeated dozens of times. Each round refines every word in light of the others.

The cost

Every word looks at every other word, so doubling the length of the text quadruples the work. This is why context windows have limits.

One more rule applies to chatbots. When predicting the next word, a word may look only backwards, at what has already been written. Anything else would be reading the answer. The lab has a switch for it.

Takeaways

What to remember

Attention is a weighted average

Match scores become shares; the word becomes the blend.

Meaning becomes contextual

The same token ends up different in different sentences.

The questions are learned

Queries, keys and values come from trained knobs, not from rules.

A transformer is this, stacked

Many heads, many layers, and a quadratic bill for long text.
