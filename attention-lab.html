Attention Lab

The lower chart is the room: every word placed by the badge it wears. The amber arrow is the question “it” is asking. Drag the arrowhead. Words the arrow points towards get a high score, the scores become shares, and the upper chart shows where the attention went. That is the entire mechanism.

Scope: one attention head, one asking word, twelve words, and queries and keys of two numbers each, set by hand so that one axis means “living thing” and the other “place”. A trained transformer learns its queries, keys and values, uses hundreds of numbers for each, runs dozens of heads in dozens of layers, and adds information about word order. None of that changes the arithmetic shown here.

What has “it” become?

After attention, “it” is replaced by the share-weighted blend of the other words. The reading is simply which side of that blend is larger.

Where does each percentage come from?

Score = question × badge, added across both numbers, divided by √2 and multiplied by the focus. Share = softmax of the scores. Nothing else is involved.

Direction is relevance

A question and a badge match when they point the same way. Learning to attend is learning which directions to ask in, and which to advertise.

Shares must add up

Softmax makes attention a competition: listening more to one word means listening less to the rest. That is how a word commits to a reading.

Chatbots cannot look ahead

Forbid looking forwards and “it” can no longer see how the sentence ends. In a real model the ambiguity is settled later, by the words that come after looking back.

Going further

The mechanism is from Vaswani et al., “Attention Is All You Need” (2017); the division by the square root of the vector length is theirs too. The example sentence follows the one used in Google's announcement of the transformer. For a careful illustrated walk through a full block, see Jay Alammar's The Illustrated Transformer. Be cautious about reading meaning into attention maps from real models: what a head attends to is suggestive, not an explanation.
