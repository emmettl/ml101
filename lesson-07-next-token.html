Lesson 07 · Language models

Predict the next token

A chatbot does one thing. Given the text so far, it produces odds for what comes next. Something picks from those odds, the pick is added to the text, and it goes round again. Every essay, poem and program a language model has written came out one piece at a time from that loop.

The one idea

Guess what comes next. Add it. Go again.

Here is the simplest machine that can do it. Take a book. To continue some text, look at its last few characters, find every place in the book where those same characters occur, and count what followed. If “rabb” was followed by “i” every single time, then after “rabb” the odds are: i, 100%. If “alice th” was followed by “o” about half the time and “a”, “i” or “e” otherwise, those are the odds. Roll a die weighted that way, write down the result, and repeat.

That is a complete language model. It has no grammar, no dictionary and no idea what a rabbit is. Its only skill is knowing what tends to follow what. The surprise is how far that one skill goes.

A language model does not look anything up and does not plan a sentence. It produces odds for the next piece, one piece at a time, from patterns in text it has seen.
Token

The piece being predicted. Here a single character; in real models a chunk of a word. See token.

Context

How much of the preceding text the model can see when it guesses.

Sampling

Picking one token from the odds. The model supplies odds; choosing is a separate step with its own settings.

Temperature

How adventurous the picking is. Low always takes the favourite. High gives long shots a real chance. See temperature.

Why context is everything

From noise, to spelling, to sentences, to plagiarism

The machine below has read the first seven chapters of Alice's Adventures in Wonderland, about 81,000 characters, and nothing else. Give it no context and it knows only which letters are common. Let it see more of what came before, a character at a time, and watch what appears. Then keep going past the point where it starts to look clever.

Around five characters of context, every word is a real word and none of the text is copied: the machine is composing, in a small way. By twelve, almost everything is lifted straight from the book. Nothing about the rule changed. With that much context each run of characters occurs only once in the book, so there is only ever one continuation on offer, and “predicting” becomes reciting. The arithmetic

Why a lookup table cannot be the answer

This machine uses 33 symbols. The number of different contexts it could be asked about grows as 33 multiplied by itself once per character: about 39 million at five characters, and more than a million million million at twelve. The book supplies 81,000 examples. Beyond a handful of characters almost every context is either unseen, or seen exactly once.

To use a long context without reciting, a model must treat contexts it has never seen as similar to ones it has. A table cannot do that. A neural network can: it is the machine from lesson 04, with knobs instead of counts, trained by descent to lower its surprise at the next token. The two lessons before this one supply what it needs to read text: a way to turn words into numbers so that similar words sit close together, and a way to let each word consult the others around it.

Same job

Odds for the next token, sample, append, repeat. Exactly this loop.

Same loss

Average surprise at what actually came next: cross-entropy from lesson 03.

Different memory

Billions of knobs that generalise, in place of a table that can only match.

Same failure, rarer

Large models also recite text they saw many times. It is memorisation, and it is why training data matters legally as well as technically.

Takeaways

What to remember

One token at a time

Odds, a pick, append, repeat. There is no plan for the sentence.

Fluency comes from context

More of the past in view turns noise into spelling into sentences.

The pick is separate from the odds

Temperature and top-k are settings on the sampler, not the model.

It continues patterns. It does not consult facts

Plausible and true are different properties. Only the first is what the loss rewards.
